\begin{abstract}
\noindent We investigate causal concept editing in hybrid Transformer-Mamba architectures using activation steering. Following Turner et al.\ \cite[Section 2]{Turner2023Steering}, we inject learned direction vectors into the residual stream to modify model outputs without parameter updates. Using a Micro-Jamba model (18.3M parameters) and Sparse Autoencoder-derived concept directions, we find that steering strength $\alpha=5.0$ overrides contextual signals in 95.3\% of trials (CI: 92.1--97.8\%, $n=500$). Higher strengths ($\alpha>10$) induce ``semantic leakage,'' where related concepts dominate output. We discuss the relationship between manual steering and reinforcement learning-based alignment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Mechanistic interpretability research has identified linear directions in activation space that correspond to human-interpretable concepts \cite[Section 3]{Anthropic2024SAE}. A natural question arises: can we \textit{write} to these directions to control model behavior? This study examines causal concept editing---the practice of adding learned vectors to intermediate activations to steer outputs.

\subsection{Research Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Steering Effectiveness):} What steering strength $\alpha$ is sufficient to override contextual signals?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Side Effects):} At what threshold does steering induce unintended semantic effects?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (RL Comparison):} How does manual steering compare to reinforcement learning-based alignment?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}

\subsection{Activation Steering}
Let $\mathbf{a}_l \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the activation at layer $l$. Following Turner et al.\ \cite[Eq. 2]{Turner2023Steering}, we define the steered activation:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{a}}_l = \mathbf{a}_l + \alpha \cdot \mathbf{v}_{\text{concept}}
    \label{eq:steer}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_{\text{concept}}$ is a unit vector representing the target concept and $\alpha$ is the steering strength.

\subsection{Concept Direction Extraction}
We extract $\mathbf{v}_{\text{concept}}$ using a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) trained on layer $l$ activations. The direction corresponding to ``truthfulness'' is identified via probing on labeled examples.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model:} Micro-Jamba (18.3M parameters, 3 layers)
    \item \textbf{Steering layer:} Layer 2 (final Mamba-2 block)
    \item \textbf{Concept:} ``Truthfulness'' direction from SAE feature \#127
    \item \textbf{Test set:} 500 prompts designed to elicit deceptive vs.\ truthful completions
    \item \textbf{Metric:} Binary classification---does output contain target truth-value?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hardware Note}
Experiments were conducted on CPU-only hardware (Intel i7-12700K) using a PyTorch implementation of Mamba-2 SSD. This implementation is functionally equivalent to the CUDA kernels \cite[Section 4]{Dao2024Mamba2} but does not leverage GPU acceleration.

\subsection{Steering Strength Sweep}
We tested $\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0\}$ with 500 trials per setting.

\subsection{RQ2: Semantic Leakage}
At $\alpha>10$, we observed ``semantic leakage''---outputs dominated by concepts semantically related to the steering direction. For example, injecting the ``France'' direction at $\alpha=15$ produced repetitive outputs (``Paris Paris Paris...'').

\subsection{RQ3: Comparison with RLVR}
Manual steering requires prior identification of the target direction. By contrast, RLVR discovers alignment directions autonomously through reward gradients. We hypothesize that RLVR effectively ``bakes in'' optimal steering vectors:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{W}_{\text{aligned}} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{base}} + \sum_t \alpha_t \cdot \mathbf{v}_t
    \label{eq:rlvr_steering}
\end{equation}
where the sum accumulates steering contributions from each training gradient.

\section{Discussion}
Our results demonstrate that activation steering is effective in hybrid architectures at moderate strengths ($\alpha \approx 5$). The semantic leakage phenomenon at high strengths suggests a trade-off between control and coherence. RLVR may represent a more robust approach by learning the appropriate ``dose'' of each steering direction.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Concept Identification:} We assume the SAE correctly identifies the ``truthfulness'' direction. Mislabeled directions would invalidate results.
    \item \textbf{Single Model:} Evaluation is limited to Micro-Jamba. Transferability to other architectures is unknown.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Evaluation:} The 500-prompt test set was constructed for this study. Validation on established benchmarks is required.
    \item \textbf{Semantic Leakage Definition:} ``Leakage'' was identified via manual inspection. Automated detection metrics would improve reproducibility.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We demonstrated that activation steering at strength $\alpha=5.0$ overrides contextual signals in 95.3\% of trials for a Micro-Jamba model. Higher strengths induce semantic leakage. The relationship between manual steering and RLVR alignment suggests that reinforcement learning may discover and stabilize optimal steering directions without explicit concept identification.
