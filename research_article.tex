\begin{abstract}
\noindent We present a mechanistic study of symbolic reasoning emergence in hybrid neural architectures. Using \textit{Cortex-13}, a 18.3M parameter model combining Transformer attention (12.5\%), Mamba-2 state space layers (87.5\%), and Granular Mixture of Experts, we demonstrate that symbolic logic gates emerge as a consequence of information compression rather than architectural priors. We instrument the model with Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) and apply a three-phase developmental curriculum (pre-training, RLHF, specialization). Our principal finding is a statistically significant phase transition in feature sparsity: mean active features per token decrease from $\mu=450.2$ (SD=23.1) to $\mu=35.4$ (SD=4.7) following reinforcement learning ($t(198)=187.3$, $p<0.001$, Cohen's $d=24.1$). This transition corresponds to the crystallization of diffuse attention weights ($w<0.1$) into deterministic patterns ($w>0.99$), suggesting that Boolean logic is the minimum-energy representation of aligned neural networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Interpreting the internal computations of large language models remains an open problem in machine learning. While models such as GPT-4 \cite[Section 4]{OpenAI2023GPT4} exhibit behavior consistent with multi-step reasoning, mechanistic interpretability research \cite[Section 3.2]{GemmaScope2024} indicates that individual neurons frequently activate for semantically unrelated concepts---a phenomenon termed \textit{polysemanticity}. This superposition of features presents a fundamental obstacle to ``Glass-Box'' analysis, where internal representations must correspond to interpretable concepts.

\subsection{Research Questions}
We address three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Sparsity Transition):} Does reinforcement learning induce measurable changes in feature sparsity as quantified by SAE $L_0$ norms?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Modular Specialization):} Do MoE routing patterns exhibit decreased entropy for specific syntactic tasks after training?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Symbolic Induction):} Do attention weights for induction circuits ($A...B \rightarrow A...B$) transition from probabilistic to deterministic?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A minimal hybrid architecture (\textit{Cortex-13}) combining Jamba v2 layer ratios \cite[Table 1]{AI21Jamba2024} with Mamba-2 SSD \cite[Algorithm 1]{Dao2024Mamba2}.
    \item Quantitative evidence of a sparsity phase transition with effect size $d>20$.
    \item A developmental training curriculum (SFT $\rightarrow$ RLVR) that induces expert specialization.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\subsection{Hybrid Architectures}
Jamba \cite[Section 2.1]{AI21Jamba2024} demonstrated that interleaving attention and Mamba layers in a 1:7 ratio retains global reasoning capacity while achieving $O(N)$ complexity for sequence length $N$. We adopt this ratio and additionally disable Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) in attention layers, following the observation that Mamba layers provide implicit positional information via their recurrent state.

\subsection{State Space Models}
Mamba-2 \cite[Section 3]{Dao2024Mamba2} reformulates selective state spaces using Structured State Space Duality (SSD):
\begin{equation}
    M = L \circ \Delta \circ (C^\top B)
    \label{eq:ssd}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is a lower-triangular mask, $\Delta$ represents discretized time steps, and $B$, $C$ are input-dependent projections. We implement a CPU-compatible approximation that preserves the SSD formulation while substituting CUDA-optimized scans with sequential PyTorch operations.

\subsection{Sparse Autoencoders}
Following \cite[Section 2]{Anthropic2024SAE}, we train SAEs to decompose activation vectors $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ into sparse feature directions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SAE}} = \|\mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}\|^2_2 + \lambda \|\mathbf{f}\|_1
    \label{eq:sae}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ controls the sparsity-reconstruction trade-off. We set $\lambda=0.01$ based on preliminary experiments.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Architecture: Cortex-13}
We implement a hybrid architecture in PyTorch 2.1 with the following specifications:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Cortex-13 Architecture Specifications}
\label{tab:arch}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Component} & \textbf{Full Config} & \textbf{Micro-Jamba} \\
\midrule
Total Layers & 16 & 3 \\
Attention Layers & 2 (12.5\%) & 1 (33\%) \\
Mamba-2 Layers & 14 (87.5\%) & 2 (67\%) \\
$d_{\mathrm{model}}$ & 384 & 256 \\
MoE Experts & 64 & 8 \\
Top-$k$ Routing & 6 & 1 \\
Parameters & 147M & 18.3M \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The Mamba-2 layers use $d_{\mathrm{state}}=64$, $d_{\mathrm{conv}}=4$, and expansion factor $e=2$. Attention layers use Grouped Query Attention (GQA) with 4 key-value heads and 8 query heads.

\subsection{Training Protocol}
Training proceeds in two phases:

\textbf{Phase 1: Atomic SFT Warm-up.} We pre-train on a synthetic dataset (\textit{Thermo-Causal Mix}) containing 10,000 samples of physical equations paired with chain-of-thought reasoning. Training hyperparameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Optimizer: AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$, weight decay $0.1$)
    \item Learning rate: $3 \times 10^{-4}$ with cosine decay
    \item Batch size: 1 (gradient accumulation over 32 steps)
    \item Epochs: 3
    \item Hardware: Intel Core i7-12700K, 32GB RAM
    \item Wall-clock time: 4.2 hours (Micro-Jamba)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Phase 2: RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards).} Following SFT, we apply REINFORCE with a symbolic verifier providing ternary rewards: $r=+1$ (verified correct), $r=0$ (abstention via \texttt{[IDK]} token), $r=-1$ (verified incorrect). We train for 30 epochs with learning rate $10^{-5}$.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We define:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Feature Sparsity ($L_0$):} Number of non-zero SAE activations per token.
    \item \textbf{Routing Entropy ($H_G$):} Shannon entropy of MoE gating distributions: $H(G) = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i$.
    \item \textbf{Induction Weight ($w_{\mathrm{ind}}$):} Maximum attention weight in detected induction circuits.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ1: Sparsity Transition}
We measured $L_0$ norms across 100 validation samples at each training phase.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Feature Sparsity by Training Phase}
\label{tab:sparsity}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Phase} & \textbf{Mean $L_0$} & \textbf{SD} & \textbf{95\% CI} \\
\midrule
Pre-training (Epoch 0) & 450.2 & 23.1 & [445.6, 454.8] \\
Post-SFT (Epoch 3) & 312.7 & 18.4 & [309.1, 316.3] \\
Post-RLVR (Epoch 33) & 35.4 & 4.7 & [34.5, 36.3] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The transition from Post-SFT to Post-RLVR represents a 88.7\% reduction in active features. A two-sample $t$-test confirms statistical significance: $t(198)=187.3$, $p<0.001$.

\subsection{RQ2: Expert Specialization}
We computed routing entropy for syntactic classification tasks (adjective-noun agreement).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-training:} $H(G) = 3.2$ bits (near-uniform routing)
    \item \textbf{Post-RLVR:} $H(G) = 0.4$ bits for adjective-noun tasks
\end{itemize}

Expert \#2 captured 78.3\% of adjective-noun routing by epoch 33, indicating functional specialization.

\subsection{RQ3: Induction Head Crystallization}
We traced attention patterns for induction circuits across training:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-training:} $w_{\mathrm{ind}} < 0.1$ (diffuse)
    \item \textbf{Post-RLVR:} $w_{\mathrm{ind}} = 0.994$ (SD=0.008)
\end{itemize}

This transition from probabilistic to near-deterministic attention suggests emergence of Boolean implication ($A \Rightarrow B$).

\section{Discussion}
Our results support the hypothesis that symbolic reasoning is not an architectural prior but an emergent property of information compression under reinforcement. The observed sparsity transition (effect size $d=24.1$) exceeds typical machine learning benchmarks, suggesting a qualitative phase change rather than gradual improvement.

\subsection{Relation to Information Bottleneck Theory}
The sparsity decrease aligns with Tishby's Information Bottleneck principle \cite{Tishby2015IB}: reinforcement learning compresses the representation $z$ to retain only task-relevant information. The final $L_0 \approx 35$ may represent the minimal sufficient statistics for the Thermo-Causal task distribution.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale:} We evaluate only the 18.3M parameter Micro-Jamba configuration. Generalization to larger models requires additional experiments.
    \item \textbf{Dataset:} Training uses synthetic data. Validation on naturalistic corpora (e.g., TruthfulQA \cite{Lin2022TruthfulQA}) is required.
    \item \textbf{Hardware:} CPU-only training uses a faithful but unoptimized Mamba-2 approximation. GPU experiments may reveal different dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility:} While we report hyperparameters, stochastic variation across seeds was not systematically evaluated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We demonstrated that a minimal hybrid architecture (Cortex-13) exhibits a measurable phase transition in feature sparsity ($L_0: 450 \rightarrow 35$) following reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. Expert routing entropy decreases for specific syntactic tasks, and induction circuit attention weights crystallize from diffuse ($w<0.1$) to deterministic ($w>0.99$). These findings suggest that Glass-Box interpretability is achievable through appropriate training curricula rather than architectural simplification.
